`symbol.lff`, and the Cyrillic and Japanese glyphs of `unicode.lff` and
`cyrillic_ii.lff`, come from the CXF fonts of QCad. In 2011 RibbonSoft
stated that the QCad 2 fonts had not been released under the GPLv2; since
2013 it releases the CXF fonts shipped with QCAD, other than the Hershey
fonts, as public domain (all copyright is waived). The Cyrillic glyphs are
Eugene Osintsev's Cyrillic font of QCad 1.4, also credited to Sam S.
Ganzha; the Japanese glyphs were drawn by Yoshimune Kobayashi for QCad 1.4.

- <https://github.com/qcad/qcad/blob/master/LICENSE.txt>

The Roman Simplex Italic font (`romansi.lff`) was made by Ain Vagula for
QCad 1.x from the Hershey Roman Simplex glyphs, slanted, with Estonian
letters added; the Hershey acknowledgement below applies to it.

The Latin glyphs of `iso.lff`, `cyrillic_ii.lff` and `unicode.lff` were
revised by Rallaz after the public-domain drawing `ISO3098.svg`.

- <https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:ISO3098.svg>

The Kochi Gothic font (`kochigothic.lff`) holds only Latin glyphs of the
Kochi Gothic font (東風ゴシック), drawn by Yasuyuki Furukawa, who described
his own Kochi outlines as equivalent to public domain. Yoshimune Kobayashi
converted them to a QCad font in 2002. It holds none of the Kochi kanji,
which were withdrawn in 2003.

- <https://web.archive.org/web/20021205031218/http://www.on.cs.keio.ac.jp/~yasu/jp_fonts.html>

The GOST outline fonts `lc_gost-outline-narrow.lff` and
`lc_gost-outline-wide.lff` were converted with ttf2lff from version 0.3 of
the OpenGost Type A and Type B TrueType fonts by Nikita Volchenkov. Until
2026 they were named `opengosttypea-regular.lff` and
`opengosttypeb-regular.lff`; they were renamed because their licence
reserves the OpenGost names, and LibreCAD still finds them under the old
names. They are released under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, and each
file carries the licence and the copyright notices of the OpenGost fonts.

The Unicode Scripts font (`unicode_scripts.lff`) holds the Thai, Lao,
Armenian, Hebrew, Ethiopic, Yi and other glyphs traced in 2026 from the
centrelines of the Noto fonts, Copyright 2022 The Noto Project Authors. It
is released under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, which does not allow
these glyphs in a GPL font, and the file carries the licence and the
notice of each Noto font it was traced from. LibreCAD draws a glyph from
it when the chosen font lacks one.

- <https://github.com/notofonts>

The Amiri font was released under the Open Font License.

- <https://www.amirifont.org/>
- <https://github.com/khaledhosny/amiri-font/blob/0.108/OFL.txt>

libraries. Qt offers them under the LGPLv3, the GPLv2 and the GPLv3;
LibreCAD, which is licensed under the GPLv2 only, uses them under the
GPLv2. The Qt libraries contain third-party code under its own licenses.
For the Qt DLLs and plugins of the Windows packages, this list gives
each component with its copyright and license text:
