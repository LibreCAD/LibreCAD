## Licenses of bundled content ##

The LibreCAD source code is released as a whole under the GPLv2.  
The following refers to the licenses used by these resources,  
when they were released independently from LibreCAD.

The SVG icons in `librecad/res/icons` were all released under the CC0 license.  

- <https://github.com/LibreCAD/Resources>
- <https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/>

libdxfrw was released under GPLv2 "or any later version".  

- <https://sourceforge.net/projects/libdxfrw/>

muParser was released under the MIT license.

- <https://beltoforion.de/article.php?a=muparser&hl=en&p=licence>

The AZOmix fonts were released under the KST32B version 2.0 license.

- <https://github.com/LibreCAD/LibreCAD/blob/master/licenses/KST32B_v2.txt>
- <http://domisan.sakura.ne.jp/article/cadfont/cadfont.html>

`symbol.lff`, and the Cyrillic and Japanese glyphs of `unicode.lff` and
`cyrillic_ii.lff`, come from the CXF fonts of QCad. In 2011 RibbonSoft
stated that the QCad 2 fonts had not been released under the GPLv2; since
2013 it releases the CXF fonts shipped with QCAD, other than the Hershey
fonts, as public domain (all copyright is waived). The Cyrillic glyphs are
Eugene Osintsev's Cyrillic font of QCad 1.4, also credited to Sam S.
Ganzha; the Japanese glyphs were drawn by Yoshimune Kobayashi for QCad 1.4.

- <https://github.com/qcad/qcad/blob/master/LICENSE.txt>

The Roman Simplex Italic font (`romansi.lff`) was made by Ain Vagula for
QCad 1.x from the Hershey Roman Simplex glyphs, slanted, with Estonian
letters added; the Hershey acknowledgement below applies to it.

The Latin glyphs of `iso.lff`, `cyrillic_ii.lff` and `unicode.lff` were
revised by Rallaz after the public-domain drawing `ISO3098.svg`.

- <https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:ISO3098.svg>

The Kochi Gothic font (`kochigothic.lff`) holds only Latin glyphs of the
Kochi Gothic font (東風ゴシック), drawn by Yasuyuki Furukawa, who described
his own Kochi outlines as equivalent to public domain. Yoshimune Kobayashi
converted them to a QCad font in 2002. It holds none of the Kochi kanji,
which were withdrawn in 2003.

- <https://web.archive.org/web/20021205031218/http://www.on.cs.keio.ac.jp/~yasu/jp_fonts.html>

The OpenGost Type A and Type B fonts (`opengosttypea-regular.lff`,
`opengosttypeb-regular.lff`) were converted with ttf2lff from version 0.3
of the OpenGost TrueType fonts by Nikita Volchenkov. They are released
under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, and each file carries the licence and
these notices:

    Copyright (c) 2011-2012, Nikita Volchenkov (<nikitavolchenkov@gmail.com>),
    with Reserved Font Name OpenGost Type A.
    Copyright (c) 2011-2012, Nikita Volchenkov (<nikitavolchenkov@gmail.com>),
    with Reserved Font Name OpenGost Type B.
    Copyright (c) 2012, Valek Filippov (<frob@gnome.org>).

- <https://github.com/LibreCAD/LibreCAD/blob/master/licenses/OFL-opengost.txt>

The glyphs added to `unicode.lff` in 2026 for Thai, Lao, Armenian, Hebrew,
Ethiopic, Yi and other scripts were traced from the centrelines of the Noto
fonts, Copyright the Noto Project Authors, under the SIL Open Font License
1.1.

- <https://github.com/notofonts>

The Amiri font was released under the Open Font License.

- <https://www.amirifont.org/>
- <https://github.com/khaledhosny/amiri-font/blob/0.108/OFL.txt>
